# Pilates & Mommy Certification
## 12-Module Curriculum Overview

**Credential:** Pilates & Mommy Certified Instructor (PMCI)
**Created by:** Dr. Niia Bishop, PhD | Alvin Ailey Trained Dancer | Pilates Certified 2007 | Creator of MAMiLATES / Mommy & Me Pilates (2009)

---

Pilates & Mommy Certification is the only credential program specifically designed for Pilates instructors who want to teach mothers and babies together — with the clinical depth, developmental knowledge, and business infrastructure to do it right. Across 12 sequential modules, you will learn everything you need to run a safe, evidence-informed, professionally structured Mommy & Me Pilates program in your community.

---

## Module 1: The Postpartum Body — A Systems Overview

The postpartum body is not simply a body that recently delivered a baby. It is a body navigating a profound hormonal recalibration, a musculoskeletal reorganization, and a shift in nearly every physiological system — all while running on fragmented sleep. This module gives you the foundational clinical fluency to teach postpartum women safely and effectively. You will learn how relaxin, prolactin, oxytocin, and estrogen directly affect the way postpartum women move, load, and recover — and why these hormonal realities demand a different approach than standard Pilates programming.

We cover the musculoskeletal adaptations that persist long after the six-week clearance visit, including lumbar hyperlordosis, thoracic kyphosis, gluteal inhibition, and scapular instability. You will leave this module able to look at a new mother moving and understand the story her body is telling — and how to respond to it.

---

## Module 2: Pelvic Floor Anatomy and Function

This module builds the anatomical foundation every postpartum Pilates instructor needs, even though you will never treat the pelvic floor — you will teach around it, with it, and toward it, and that requires knowing what you are working with. We cover the levator ani, coccygeus, and urogenital triangle in accessible, instructor-level detail, and then we go deeper into the question that matters most in practice: is this pelvic floor too weak, or too tight?

The distinction between hypertonic (overactive) and hypotonic (underactive) pelvic floor dysfunction is the most important clinical concept in postpartum fitness. Getting it wrong — prescribing Kegels to a hypertonic floor — causes harm. This module gives you the screening language, the referral criteria, and the communication tools to identify which students need a pelvic floor physical therapist before they need a Pilates progression.

---

## Module 3: Diastasis Recti — Assessment and Rehabilitation

Diastasis recti is present in the majority of postpartum women at some level, and it remains unaddressed in the majority of postpartum fitness settings. This module changes that. You will learn to guide your students through a practical self-assessment, understand what gap width and linea alba tension mean functionally, and apply a six-tip management framework in every class you teach.

We cover the exercises that are truly contraindicated for active diastasis — and the ones that are not. We lay out the three most effective exercises for DR rehabilitation, walk through the progressive return to full Pilates repertoire, and clarify exactly when a student is ready to advance. By the end of this module, you will never again wonder whether your abdominal cueing is helping or hurting.

---

## Module 4: Cesarean Recovery

One in three babies born in the United States arrives by cesarean section — which means one in three of your students is recovering from major abdominal surgery, not just childbirth. This module covers the anatomy of the cesarean incision, the multi-layer healing timeline, and the specific movement contraindications that apply in the weeks and months following a C-section.

We address scar tissue management — what it is, what it affects, and how to speak with your students about it without overstepping your scope of practice. You will receive communication templates for coordinating with OB providers, and you will learn how to welcome students into class before they are cleared for full participation in a way that is inclusive, structured, and safe.

---

## Module 5: Breastfeeding and Movement

A breastfeeding mother is operating under a hormonal profile that is physiologically distinct from a mother who has weaned — and that distinction matters for how you design, cue, and modify her movement experience. This module covers the role of prolactin in sustained ligament laxity, the caloric and hydration demands of lactation, and the postural damage that accumulates from weeks and months of nursing without intervention.

We give you a specific, practical framework for modifying class for breastfeeding students: what to do when a student arrives engorged, how to handle nursing during class without disrupting the group, how to address wrist pain, and how to prioritize the anterior chain work that breastfeeding mothers desperately need. We also cover the language of normalization — creating a class culture where feeding your baby during a squat is unremarkable and expected.

---

## Module 6: Biomechanics of Daily Motherhood and Anterior Chain Work

Fitness programs address what happens in the studio. This module addresses what happens everywhere else — which, for new mothers, is where the real postural damage accumulates. We examine the biomechanics of baby-carrying, infant lifting, diaper changes, car seat transfers, and the sustained nursing posture that most mothers hold for hours per day without any body awareness support.

The centerpiece of this module is the anterior fascial line and the axillary (armpit) complex — a region that is systematically overloaded and almost universally overlooked in postpartum programming. The pectoralis minor, serratus anterior, and subscapularis are all compromised by baby-holding and nursing mechanics, and when they are not addressed, they limit shoulder stability, impair overhead movement, and create the chronic neck and shoulder pain that many mothers accept as inevitable. We teach you release techniques for the chest, anterior shoulder, and axillary musculature that you can incorporate into every warm-up. We also introduce the Bolster: Dr. Bishop's proprioceptive anterior abdominal repositioning tool, and the technique for using it safely in class.

---

## Module 7: Core Rehabilitation Progressions

This is the technical heart of the certification. You already know how to teach Pilates — this module teaches you how to teach Pilates to a body that is rebuilding its core from the inside out. We start with the breath-first principle: the understanding that diaphragmatic breathing is not a warm-up placeholder but the actual intervention, because the pelvic floor and diaphragm must restore their coordinated rhythm before any meaningful core loading is safe or effective.

From there we walk through a five-stage progressive loading continuum that moves from supine neurological re-education through full Pilates repertoire return. Each stage is defined by functional readiness criteria, not a calendar. You will learn to see where a student is in the continuum and to progress her at the rate her body — not her six-week clearance date — dictates. The three-system coordination cue (exhale, lift, and load) that anchors this module will become the most useful teaching tool in your Pilates career.

---

## Module 8: Infant and Toddler Development Foundations

Great Mommy & Me class design is not an accident — it is the product of understanding why babies and toddlers respond the way they do, and engineering the class environment to meet them there. This module covers the developmental science behind every element of the Pilates & Mommy class format: why the high-contrast cards are placed near newborns, why rattles serve a neurological function, why the baby massage is clinically grounded rather than decorative, and why the circle setup supports social-emotional development in ways no other arrangement does.

We walk through motor, visual, auditory, and social milestones from 3 months to 36 months, so you can look at a room full of babies at different stages and know how to adapt every activity for every child present. We introduce the baby sign language curriculum (pre-verbal communication reduces frustration, deepens the mother-child bond, and differentiates your class from anything else in your market). And we present the full baby massage stroke sequence — narrated, developmental, and designed to be the most memorable moment of every class. We also introduce Dr. Bishop's original Baby Movement Curriculum — Level I (Discovery) and Level II (Awareness) — protected intellectual property developed through fifteen years of direct Mommy & Me instruction.

---

## Module 9: Psychological and Emotional Dimensions

The Mommy & Me class may be the first consistent social environment a new mother enters after birth. That means your room will hold women navigating postpartum depression, postpartum anxiety, postpartum rage, birth trauma, profound identity disruption, and the relentless emotional weight of early motherhood — often without realizing they need support. This module does not train you to be a therapist. It trains you to be a safe, informed presence who can recognize what she is seeing, respond without amplifying, and refer with warmth and specificity.

We cover the full spectrum of postpartum mood disorders — including the frequently underdiagnosed presentations of postpartum anxiety and postpartum rage — and give you screening language and referral frameworks that are clear, compassionate, and appropriate to your scope. We address body image and postpartum identity with nuance: how to create a class culture where mothers are working toward what their bodies can do, not apologizing for what they look like. And we introduce the concept of regulated co-presence — how a well-facilitated Mommy & Me class can support maternal nervous system regulation without ever crossing into therapy.

---

## Module 10: Building the Mommy & Me Class

This module is where everything comes together. We walk you through the complete operational reality of running a Mommy & Me Pilates class: the circle setup protocol step by step, the music curation and licensing framework, the art of teaching mixed-age groups where the youngest participant is three months and the oldest is nearly three years, and the management strategies for every piece of class unpredictability — the wandering toddler, the crying newborn, the nursing mother, the moment when two babies decide to cry simultaneously.

You will receive the full 90-minute session arc in timed detail, with notes on how to adapt each segment when the room needs something different than the plan. We go deep on the baby massage closing ritual — how to narrate it, how to frame it for families, how to make it the consistent, sacred closing chapter that mothers come to count on. By the end of this module, you are ready to teach.

---

## Module 11: Business Model and Pricing

The credential without the business model is an incomplete tool. This module gives you the financial architecture to build a Pilates & Mommy practice that is profitable, sustainable, and priced in a way that reflects the true value of what you offer. We present two business models in detail: the own-your-space model (high margin, low overhead, seasonal cohort structure) and the rental model (flexible, accessible, with a minimum-enrollment policy that protects your time and income).

We break down the three revenue layers available to every PMCI instructor — cohort classes, private postnatal sessions, and the certification referral affiliate relationship — and provide the enrollment language, pricing frameworks, and policy structures that transform a class into a professional program. The foundational principle of this module is simple: you are not selling Pilates classes. You are selling a completely curated, safe, fully-equipped experience for mother and baby. Everything handled. Just show up. Price accordingly.

---

## Module 12: Certification Completion and Practicum

The final module guides you through the completion requirements for the PMCI credential: a comprehensive written exam, a 20-minute video practicum submission, and a practical space and readiness checklist. The practicum is designed to be achieved in your actual teaching environment — not a theoretical assessment, but a demonstration that you can deliver the Pilates & Mommy experience to real families.

We also present the continuing education pathway that defines where PMCI graduates go next: infant massage instructor certification, baby sign language instructor training, structured collaboration with pelvic floor physical therapists, and more. The PMCI is not a destination — it is the foundation of a specialty that will distinguish your practice, serve your community, and grow with you.
